## "Granny, get on!"

The instructor went to secondary school by train from Dolný Kubín to Tvrdošín — an hour on the Orava line. There's a joke about how fast that line is: the conductor shouts "Granny, get on!" and the granny replies "No, I can't, son, I'm in a hurry."

Normally a locomotive and two carriages came. It was critical when a locomotive with one carriage came. Once, though, **only the locomotive** arrived — everyone was very close to each other. The service was provided, under strange conditions — exactly like horizontal scaling when one bus fails.

## Buses with trailers

A bigger bus isn't a new idea. Under communism there were buses with a covered trailer hitched behind — the forerunner of today's articulated buses. Vertical scaling in practice.

## Weekend outages

Availability statistics give figures like 99.9 % a year. The contrast: the state portal announces maintenance for the whole Saturday and the train-ticket app reports an outage. Exactly what happens when everything runs on one server.

> "We don't work for the government, we work for business. We can't afford outages of a day or two. Only the state can provide that kind of embarrassment."

## The helmsman

The instructor wasn't sure whether Kubernetes means "to steer" or "a ship's wheel". The Greek *kybernétés* is the **helmsman** — the one who steers the ship. Hence the wheel in the logo and the word *cybernetics*.

## Kubernetes: The Documentary

Anyone who wants to know how Kubernetes came about, what problems they solved, and who the first customers were should watch the two-part **Kubernetes: The Documentary** by Honeypot (each part about an hour). "A really nice thing, the story behind it is really good." Kubernetes caught on despite how it all started.

## How the distribution was chosen

The instructor started the trainings with **MicroK8s** from Canonical — back then every participant had Windows installed differently and MicroK8s had an installer for it. Later he tried **K3d** (K3s in Docker, several nodes on one machine), but networking ports added extra work. He also considered **Rancher**, but it wanted at least 4 GB of RAM. Today the course uses K3s on EC2 machines.

The university's **Arena** system (testing students' assignments in containers, see the [Docker wisdoms](/devops-notes/en/docker/mudrosti/historia/)) is about to migrate to Kubernetes. Testing an assignment is a textbook **Job**: an isolated environment, run the tests, generate a report, finish.
