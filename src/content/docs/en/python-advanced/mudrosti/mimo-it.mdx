## Push-to-talk in Google Meet

## MicroPython and testing hardware

Mirek is working out how to test (with pytest, in CI) code in **MicroPython** — a lean implementation of Python for microcontrollers:

- MicroPython needs roughly **256 kB of flash and 16 kB of RAM** at minimum — a classic Arduino (2 kB RAM, 32 kB flash) is not enough, a Raspberry Pi Pico (~150 MHz) is,
- how do you test in CI that the thermometer reports 100 °C and the device raises an alarm? “You stick it in the kettle” — that will not work in GitHub Actions,
- real products deal with the battery (it cannot run 24/7), connection outages (it must not freeze on an exception — there is no Docker to restart it), and Wi-Fi (an SSID and password hard-coded in the source is no solution).

Tutorials about blinking an LED are “baby projects” — a professional view is a completely different kind of coding and thinking. “Lego for grown-ups.”

## Donkey and the night-train attendant

Polling is Donkey from Shrek 2: “Are we there yet? When will we get there?” Notification is the attendant on a night train — they take your ticket and wake you half an hour before your station, however late the train is. An alarm clock (cron) would wake you somewhere at the border. More in the [Assets](/devops-notes/en/python-advanced/assety/#polling-vs-notification) chapter.

## Jokes by number

A newcomer joins a group of jokers. Someone says “7” and everyone roars with laughter; “17” — an explosion of laughter. A neighbour explains: everyone knows the jokes already, so they save time and just say the numbers. The newcomer tries “9” — silence. He tries “7” — silence again. “Why is nobody laughing?” — “Well, you just can't tell them.”

## Airflow for 800 students

At the university where Mirek teaches, Airflow is being prepared to grade student assignments in C programming. A DAG for one student is easy — but there are 800+ students. Making 800 identical DAGs that differ only in a parameter? That is what **dynamic task mapping** is for (day 5).
